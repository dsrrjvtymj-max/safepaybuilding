How to break your project into milestone tranches

Every SafePay Building project runs on tranches — chunks of the total price, each released only when a specific, verifiable stage of work is signed off. Getting the split right protects everyone. Here's how.

Why tranches matter

A single upfront deposit is exactly what turns a home improvement project into a Citizens Advice statistic* — it hands over risk before any work exists to show for it. A single "pay it all on completion" arrangement swings the risk the other way, leaving a contractor funding materials and labour for months with nothing back. Tranches split the difference: each one is small enough that neither side is ever badly exposed, and tied to something concrete enough that both sides can agree it happened.

Four principles for splitting a project

1️⃣

Tie every tranche to a verifiable point of completion — not a date, not a percentage of "effort." "Foundation poured and building-control signed off" is verifiable. "Week 3 of works" is not.

2️⃣

Never let a tranche get ahead of the work. If the cumulative amount released so far could ever exceed the value of work actually completed, you've recreated the upfront-deposit problem one instalment at a time.

3️⃣

Size early tranches to real cash outlay, not labour. A materials-heavy stage (timber, a kitchen order, roofing materials) can fairly justify a larger tranche than the labour hours alone would suggest — the contractor is genuinely paying suppliers upfront.

4️⃣

Hold back a final retention tranche of 5–10% for snagging and the final walkthrough. It's the single biggest lever homeowners have to get small defects fixed promptly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Front-loading a tranche before the equivalent work — or materials spend — actually exists
  • Only two tranches ("half now, half later"), which recreates most of the upfront-deposit risk
  • Vague descriptions like "kitchen work" instead of "units and worktops fitted and inspected"
  • No final retention tranche held back for snagging
  • Tranches that don't match how the contractor actually pays suppliers and labour
